## What the Ambrose research changed

The approved outline cold open stays Promontory (Stanford misses; the wire says "done"). Council Bluffs is a full middle scene, not a replacement open.

- Porch, August 1859: Pacific House, Dodge at 28, Pusey, the long legs, "Dodge, what's the best route," Platte Valley, forty-second parallel, "He shelled my woods completely." Seventeen Riddle Tract lots, $3,500, roughly $130,000. Steamboat from St. Joseph. Lewis and Clark's bluff. "Not one, but many railroads will center here." (Ambrose, *Nothing Like It in the World*, pp. 22–23, 37–38.)
- Railroad lawyer: first campaign at 23, eighth of thirteen, the Illinois Central bill ($2,000, the Webster line, re-billed $5,000, sued and won), the *Effie Afton* / Rock Island bridge, 1857. (Ambrose pp. 25–29.) Rayner's "manifest destiny" line is attributed to Rayner, not folded into Ambrose.
- Slavery blocks the route; secession unblocks it. Davis and the 32nd parallel, Gadsden at $10 million with no CPI conversion, the camels as a detail, Judah's 1857 pamphlet, Buchanan's veto threat, the empty 1860 calendar, Russell, and Dodge's "military necessity" plus holding the Pacific Coast. (Ambrose pp. 30, 58–60, 67, 74–78; Chang ch. 3.)
- July 1, 1862, signed during Malvern Hill. House 79–49 on May 6, Senate 35–5 on June 20, House concurrence noted. Judah's telegram performed: "We have drawn the elephant. Now let us see if we can harness him." (Ambrose pp. 78–80.)
- Morrill Act the next day, July 2, 1862. New York: 31 representatives + 2 senators under the 1860 census = 33 × 30,000 = 990,000 acres, about 120 times today's Stanford campus (8,353 acres). Cornell, chartered 1865, is New York's land-grant. The script says aloud that Stanford is not one.
- Arcade Creek. Josiah Whitney, not Asa. About 15 miles, $16,000 × 15 = $240,000, roughly $6.5 million (1863 index 37), about 570 years at $35 a month. Sargent's line is performed. Ambrose's parenthetical, that another report puts a variant in Lincoln's mouth, is said aloud. The script does not claim Lincoln said it. (Ambrose p. 107.) Stanford's September 29, 1862 telegram phrase is the outline's documented line.
- Elm Grove hedged as legend. Memorial says "Mr. Whitney." Leland at thirteen is about 1837, before the China merchant's trip (1842–44) and campaign (from 1844). The 1849 book quotation is performed as the document. Benton's "talk, trade, and marry" is one sentence against Stanford's inaugural.
- Crocker's pistol at Green River, June 1850, performed. Strobridge's ax handle (Ambrose p. 163) is discipline of Chinese workers, not recruiting. Bloomer Cut costs him the eye. "I will not boss Chinese," then he did.
- Health seekers are two sentences at the hook. Blake 1852 and Praslow are inside the episode's years. The quarter-of-arrivals figure is given for 1840–1900, not shoved into 1869–1900. The 1870 "sanatorium of the world" line and the post-railroad wave are marked as later. No Pasadena, no parrot, no Leland Jr. death scene.

## Pacing

8,895 narrated words, about 59.3 minutes. The Elephant section is the long teach (the act, the rates, Morrill, the 1864 amendment) and it is where the new research had to land. Vigilance is the short scene, about a minute and three quarters, so the porch and the streets sidebar could exist without compressing the strike or the ten-mile day.
