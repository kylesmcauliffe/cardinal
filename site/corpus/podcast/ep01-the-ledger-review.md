## What changed in the review pass

- Party size set at sixty-four, the standard count of the group that left San Diego on July 14, 1769 (Monterey County Historical Society; NPS). A complete name roll was never settled, so the script says "by the standard count."
- November 4, 1769 named as the feast of San Carlos Borromeo, from the expedition diary as printed by Eldredge: Mass on the beach, then the view from the summit. The summit is named Sweeney Ridge (NPS / Golden Gate National Recreation Area).
- San Carlos scene added. Lieutenant Juan Manuel de Ayala, night of August 5, 1775; forty-four days; pilot José de Cañizares drew the chart because Ayala was recovering from a pistol wound in his right foot (Ayala's own report, in Eldredge). Isla de los Ángeles / Ayala Cove. Landmark 236 at Aquatic Park, Beach and Larkin. Departure for Monterey on September 18, arrival the 19th.
- Town of San Carlos: the city's own site says the name is lore, three candidates (the ship, Carlos III, the feast day). Script says the record confirms all three existed and does not say which one the developers meant.
- El Camino Real as State Route 82. Interstate 280 as the Junipero Serra Freeway. Interstate 380 was the Portola Freeway and is now the Quentin L. Kopp Freeway.
- Yerba Buena ordinance, January 30, 1847, Washington Bartlett, quoted in substance from the San Francisco Museum text. Benicia sequel from the California State Library's Celebrate California note.
- Fourth-grade sugar-cube missions updated. The California History–Social Science Framework says building missions from sugar cubes or popsicle sticks does not help students understand the period and is offensive to many. The line is performed.
- Serra biography filled in: born Miguel José, November 24, 1713, Petra, Mallorca; university chair given up in 1749; died August 28, 1784 at Mission San Carlos Borromeo, Carmel, nine missions founded; buried there. Canonization September 23, 2015, paraphrased, not a possibly inexact papal quotation. Stanford: announcement September 13, 2018; Jane Stanford Way effective October 7, 2019; Serra Street kept.
- Class size updated to 1,866 first-years enrolled in fall 2025 (Class of 2029, Stanford facts page). Campus updated to 8,353 acres (facts.stanford.edu).
- Senate vote on the treaty left out. National Archives milestone page says 34–14; other official tellings say 38–14. The deletion of Article X is not in dispute.
- Protocol of Querétaro, May 26, 1848, added, with the grants sentence performed from the protocol text.
- Land Act hook: "more than eight hundred claims" (both the Bancroft finding aid and Hornbeck's 1979 article say 813). The script does not pick 514 versus 604 confirmed, because those sources split the appeals differently. It says most were confirmed and confirmation was not the same as keeping the land. Buelna's widow: claim 1853, patent 1868, George Gordon, carried from the land-case summaries in the draft.
- Astor share-of-GDP number left for Episode 2's method lesson. CPI only: about $20 million is roughly $770 million.
- Dana, Harris, Royce, Trist, Grant, and Article XII are performed from the texts in hand (Dana and Royce and Harris and Brechin in Kyle's library; Trist and Article XII from the National Archives milestone page).

## Decided

Bancroft Land Case 129 ND: Governor Alvarado granted the Rinconada del Arroyo de San Francisquito on February 16, 1841, to María Antonia Mesa, Rafael Soto's widow. Rafael had settled there around 1835 and died in 1839. The script names Mesa as grantee and dates the grant in 1841, as it dates the other grants. Buelna's Rancho San Francisquito, Peña's Rincón de San Francisquito, and the Soto Rinconada stay distinct. The Seale lawyer story stays off the air. "Roughly, downtown Palo Alto" is unchanged.
