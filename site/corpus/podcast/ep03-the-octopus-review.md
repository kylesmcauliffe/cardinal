## Changes from the outline

- Political spending of about $700,000 is placed in **1884** (White's special session), not 1882. Grocery-dollar figure is about **$26 million**, not $24 million.
- Mussel Slough dead are **seven on the day, eight if Haymaker is counted**. The outline said seven. Both numbers are spoken.
- Spite fence is **forty feet** in Rayner and OpenSFHistory. Thirty feet is identified as a later telling. George's "veritable coffin" is not used.
- Hearst's Senate seat is an **1886 appointment plus an 1887 term**, not a single "elected 1886."
- The $1.25 figure is taught as the **1862 Act's ceiling**, beside the company's own **$2.50-and-up** advertisement, which is the number the settlers actually had.
- Exclusion is not called the first immigration law. The **Page Act of 1875** is named.
- March 1884 hook does **not** call Stanford a senator. He entered the Senate in March 1885.

## Cut on purpose

- Henry George calling the fence a "veritable coffin." Not found.
- Stoneman's "natural person / artificial person" lines from the cover-story script. Not relocated in White or Winkler.
- The $20 founding loan for the *Chronicle*. Not on the paper's history page used here.
- Dramatized details of how Charles de Young ambushed Kalloch. Accounts conflict.
- Artist's first name on *The Curse of California*. Rayner says Edward Keller; other accounts say G. Frederick Keller.
- A Yale sentence. No verified Yale crossing in this episode's years. The Boston Forbes / China-trade beat is the East Coast beat instead.
- Share-of-economy conversions. Left blank until MeasuringWorth is pulled.
- A closing catalogue of the hour. The last minutes are the Florence scene and the hook only.
- "No other state had ten thousand" Chinese residents. Soft secondary; the census ratios carry the point.

## Critique pass

- Cut the narrated refusal to invent Florence's last words or the telegram. The death stays: March 13, 1884, age fifteen, Roman fever in Jordan and typhoid in the university histories.
- The hook no longer reads a grant clause. Episode 4 owns "sell or encumber."
- Bierce's "hands out of his pockets" line is spoken once, in the Examiner section.
- The 1862 line is split the way Episode 2 splits it. Inaugural: "the dregs of her population," "a degraded and distinct people." Campaign: "the dregs of Asia." Not one fused sentence.
- Each dollar figure keeps one comparison. Grocery dollars for the house, the $200,000 letter, the fence, the $700,000, and the $24 hog case. The acre prices are compared to the company's own $2.50 advertisement, not to a second modern yardstick.
- "Here is the concept" / "That is the concept" are gone. The explanations of the land rise, regulatory capture, and scapegoating stay.
- Dewey Monument date is not in this episode.

## Decided

The April 2, 1881 *Wasp* verse read on air is the first of three stanzas, not the last. The same column calls Crow, the railroad-side buyer killed at Mussel Slough, a hero, and one narrator line says so. Citation is the free Internet Archive scan of *The Wasp*, vol. 6 (Jan.–June 1881): https://archive.org/details/waspvol61881unse. Narrated words after that line: **8,710**, about **58.1 minutes**.
