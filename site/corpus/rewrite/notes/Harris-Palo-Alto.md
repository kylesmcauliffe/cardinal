# Cardinal Notes — Harris, Palo Alto

The moments below are in reading order, with the earliest chapter first. More will be appended as reading continues.

- (Intro (text00007)) “The children of California shall be our children,” Leland Stanford told his wife, Jane, when they decided to build Palo Alto.
  - Why: A vow at the founding that the whole series pays off: the institution claims the future's kids. Episode 4.
- (Section III frontispiece) Poster: “Strike Against the Stanford Empire” (1970), April Third Movement archive.
  - Why: A ready-made title card. Episode 6.
- (Section IV frontispiece) Security fences built around HP’s Deer Creek lab after a 1976 bomb attack (Measure, Aug–Sept 1976).
  - Why: Suburban tech behind fences: tension in a sunny frame. Episode 7.
- (Ch 1.2) During the 1877 crisis, workers “marched up to Leland Stanford’s mansion and threatened him.” Kearney: “I will give the Central Pacific just three months to discharge their Chinamen.”
  - Why: A mob at the Nob Hill door. Episode 3.
- (Ch 1.3) Stanford rehired Muybridge in 1876, “apparently not begrudging Helios the murder and child abandonment” in between.
  - Why: The man who invents cinema on the farm had just killed his wife's lover. Episode 4. [DEEP CUT]
- (Ch 1.3 “Blood That Trots”) Paraphrase: Muybridge photographs Stanford's trotters at the Palo Alto farm to settle whether all four hooves leave the ground. It's the first motion picture.
  - Why: A frozen horse in mid-air: the farm literally invents cinema. Episode 4.
- (Ch 1.3) “On March 13, 1884, two months before his sixteenth birthday, the Stanfords’ only child died.”
  - Why: A fever room in Florence: the single death that creates the university. Episode 3→4 (end turn).
- (Ch 2.1 “Local Ghosts”) The chapter subtitle reads “The Almost Certain Murder of [redacted-style asterisks]”.
  - Why: Harris's playful censoring is itself a scene beat for Jane's death. Episode 4. [contested; one-sentence treatment]
- (Ch 2.3) When Hoover had typhoid as a junior, his professor Branner “even paid his favorite pupil’s medical bills.”
  - Why: The orphan is adopted by the institution. Episode 5.
- (Ch 2.3 “Hooverville”) Paraphrase: Hoover, born 1874, is in Stanford's pioneer class, then a mining engineer in Western Australia and China.
  - Why: An orphan boy turning into a global mining fixer. Episode 5.
- (Ch 2.4) Paraphrase: Terman, the “genius son” of the IQ inventor; internment of the Santa Clara Japanese in the same chapter.
  - Why: The father ranks children; the son builds the industry. Intercut with families taken away. Episode 5.
- (Ch 3.1 “Space Settlers”) “The first lease at the Stanford Industrial Park was granted to Varian’s R & D department in 1953—no surprise, because Terman was an investor and board…”
  - Why: The insider signature on the first lease. Episode 6.
- (Ch 3.2 “The Solid State”) Shockley’s recruits, “a team Shockley later tarred as ‘the traitorous eight.’”
  - Why: A betrayal origin myth with a villain's own nickname. Episode 6.
- (Ch 4.2 “War Capitalism”) Chapter topic: “The Office of Technology Licensing and the Privatization of the Cold War University.”
  - Why: The licensing office as the new ticket booth. Episode 7.
