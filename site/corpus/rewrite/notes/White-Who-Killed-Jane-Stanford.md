# Cardinal Notes — White, Who Killed Jane Stanford?

The moments below are in reading order, with the earliest chapter first. More will be appended as reading continues.

- (Ch 17, p. 101) On the night of Nov 14, Jordan visits historian George Howard and tells him the faculty should “keep still.”
  - Why: A night visit to silence a professor. Episode 4.
- (Ch 28, p. 167) “On February 15, 1905, roughly a month after the Poland Spring Water incident, Bertha Berner and a maid, May Hunt, accompanied Jane Stanford on the steamship Korea…”
  - Why: A second attempt follows the first; departure by ship. Episode 4.
- (Ch 29, p. 173) “Oh God, forgive me my sins… Is my soul prepared to meet my dear ones?… This a horrible death to die.”
  - Why: Last words. The series' strongest cold open. Episode 4 (cold open).
- (Ch 36, p. 208) “…the combination of strychnine in both Mrs. Stanford’s body and the bicarbonate seemed conclusive. She had been poisoned.”
  - Why: The chemists' verdict. Episode 4. [the murder is contested (Jordan argued heart failure); one sentence]
- (Ch 38, p. 217) “By the time David Starr Jordan and Timothy Hopkins reached Honolulu, it was apparent that all the suspects were lying.”
  - Why: A noir line. Episode 4.
- (Ch 43, p. 248) On Mar 15, the day Jordan left with the body, “Tay Wang” wrote to Jordan asking for a meeting. “It is the strangest piece of evidence…”
  - Why: A mystery letter at the dock. Episode 4.
- (Ch 45, p. 266) The choir sings “Nearer, My God, to Thee,” and the casket goes into the steel chest in the mausoleum.
  - Why: Episode-ending image. Episode 4 (end).
- (Notes ch 16) Ross's offending speech was reported as “Warning Against Coolie ‘Natives’ and Japanese” (Call, May 8, 1900).
  - Why: Jane fired a professor for attacking the Chinese labor her husband's railroad used. Episode 4. [DEEP CUT]
- (Notes ch 36, p. 339) Newspaper headline: “Mrs. Stanford Died from Natural Causes” (Examiner, Mar 14, 1905).
  - Why: The institution's counter-story in a headline. Episode 4. [contested]
