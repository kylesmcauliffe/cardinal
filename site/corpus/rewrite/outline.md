# Cardinal — Documentary Rewrite Outline
*Eight episodes, one story. Planning document only. No prose has been drafted. Oct 13, 2026.*

## Frame and defaults
- **Form:** eight episodes of a documentary series. Each has a cold open, a through-line and an end turn or cliffhanger. Time only moves forward. People are followed through scenes.
- **Narrator:** all-knowing. The narrator already knows how it ends and speaks with a detective's or time-traveler's calm. It tells the story as established fact, with no prosecution-versus-defense staging. Where the record is contested, the narrator settles it in one confident sentence, cites it, and moves on.
- **Thesis held back:** the "chokepoint → institution → succession" pattern is never stated before Episode 8. Readers should notice it by Episode 4. The line "Rail controlled movement. Stanford controls succession." appears once, late.
- **Tone model:** *O.J.: Made in America*.
- ASSUMPTION: the reader is a general nonfiction reader.
- ASSUMPTION: Jane Stanford's death is presented as strychnine poisoning (the inquest found that). The murder question gets one sentence, e.g. "The coroner's jury named poison; Stanford's own president named the heart; the poison is the one the chemists found."
- ASSUMPTION: Bohemian Grove and remote viewing are cut unless well sourced. Denton (*The Profiteers*) sources Nixon/Bechtel at the Grove, so it's allowed as one detail in Ep 7, not a theme.
- ASSUMPTION: Dawn stays a separate book, with at most a one-page prologue nod (the Gálvez street sign).
- ASSUMPTION: keep Kyle's best lines from the current draft, e.g. "It was never a secret. It was only subliminal."; the street-sign device; "Rail controlled movement…".
- ASSUMPTION: Chicago author-date citations, keeping the repo's references.bib.
- ASSUMPTION: the story ends in 2025.
- ASSUMPTION: delivery is a Google Doc plus a PDF.
- **Scale:** ~8 episodes × 4–6 chapters ≈ 40 chapters, ~3,000 words each ≈ 120k words.
- **Repo paths** are relative to `books/cardinal/`.
- **Source shorthand:** Harris = *Palo Alto* (2023); White-J = *Who Killed Jane Stanford?* (2022); White-R = *Railroaded* (2011); Rayner = *The Associates* (2008); Ambrose = *Nothing Like It in the World* (2000); Chang = *Ghosts of Gold Mountain*; Berlin = *Troublemakers* (2017); Baker = *How to Rule the World* (2026); Denton = *The Profiteers* (2016); Levy = *In the Plex* (2011); Siddiqui = *Hubris Maximus* (2025); Winkler = *We the Corporations*; Madley = *An American Genocide*.

## Episode 1 — The Ledger (1769–1848)
- **Cold open:** Nov 1769. Portolá's men camp beneath a lone double-trunked redwood on San Francisquito Creek; a soldier marks it in a diary. Open on the tree, the "tall stick."
- **Through-line:** land becomes paper; whoever keeps the record owns the ground.
- **Scenes:**
  1. *The Tree.* POV: a Portolá expedition diarist (Crespí). Place: San Francisquito Creek. Date: Nov 1769. Opening image: the redwood against an empty plain.
  2. *Baptismal Register.* POV: a Franciscan at Mission Santa Clara, plus an Ohlone neophyte as the counter-POV. Date: 1777–1800s. Opening image: an ink entry giving a new Christian name.
  3. *Paper State.* POV: a presidio commandant writing to Mexico City. Place: Monterey / SF Presidio. Date: 1790s–1821. Opening image: a letter that takes a year to be answered.
  4. *Rancho.* POV: the Soto family (Rancho San Francisquito). Date: 1839. Opening image: a diseño, a hand-drawn grant map using oaks and creek bends as boundaries.
  5. *Nine Days.* POV: James Marshall at Coloma, intercut with treaty signers at Guadalupe Hidalgo. Date: Jan 24 / Feb 2, 1848. Opening image: a flake of gold in a mill race.
- **Repo:** ch/2-01-mission-system.qmd, 2-02-presidio-paper-state.qmd, 2-03-thin-sovereignty.qmd, 2-04-secularization-rancho.qmd, 2-05-war-paperwork.qmd.
- **Sources:** Harris ch 1.1; Madley; Brechin ch 1; the Crespí diary (primary, needs sourcing); White-R ch 1.
- **End turn:** the border moves nine days after gold, and nobody at the table knows.
- **Reader realizes, without being told:** the first owners of this land held it by record-keeping, not by settlement.

## Episode 2 — The Toll (1848–1869)
- **Cold open:** May 10, 1869, Promontory Summit. Leland Stanford swings at the last spike and misses; the telegraph sends "DONE" anyway (Ambrose ch 17).
- **Then:** cut back 20 years.
- **Through-line:** shopkeepers learn the money is in the passage, not the gold.
- **Scenes:**
  1. *Panama.* POV: Collis Huntington stranded crossing Panama. Date: 1849. Opening image: "eight to ten deaths a day" (Rayner ch 2).
  2. *The Store.* POV: Leland Stanford, a Sacramento grocer. Date: 1852–56. Opening image: a ledger of shovels and flour.
  3. *Vigilance.* POV: a merchant on the Committee at Fort Gunnybags. Date: 1856. Opening image: sandbags on Sacramento Street. Repo 2-07 is strong here.
  4. *The Surveyor.* POV: Theodore Judah. Date: 1860–63. Opening image: Judah's letter, "I have struck a lucky streak" (Rayner notes ch 3). Judah dies of yellow fever after Panama, sidelined.
  5. *Vacant Chairs.* POV: Lincoln's Congress. Date: July 1, 1862. Opening image: empty Southern desks.
  6. *The Summit.* POV: Chinese crews and J. H. Strobridge. Date: 1866–67. Opening image: tunnel faces under snow, and the strike of late May 1867, "the hardest blow we have had" (Ambrose ch 11).
  7. *Ten Miles.* POV: Crocker. Date: Apr 28, 1869. Opening image: the $10,000 bet with Durant (Ambrose ch 16/17).
- **Repo:** ch/2-06-gold-incentive-shock.qmd, 2-07-statehood-private-governance.qmd, 2-08-vacant-chairs.qmd, 2-09-railroads-first-monopoly.qmd.
- **Sources:** Rayner chs 2–8; Ambrose chs 1, 11, 16–17; Chang; White-R chs 1–3; Harris ch 1.2.
- **End turn:** back at the spike. The bells ring nationwide, and the Contract and Finance Company books are already being kept where no one will see them.
- **Reader realizes:** public land and bonds built a private toll road, and the men who built it now own the passage.

## Episode 3 — The Octopus (1869–1884)
- **Cold open:** May 11, 1880, Mussel Slough. Settlers and railroad-backed buyers meet in a wheat field; seven men die (Rayner ch 13).
- **Through-line:** the toll becomes government, law and race policy.
- **Scenes:**
  1. *Nob Hill.* POV: the four Associates building mansions. Date: 1870s. Opening image: a cable car climbing to Stanford's door.
  2. *Mussel Slough.* POV: a tenant farmer. Date: 1878–80. Opening image: the railroad's land prices posted at $17–$40 an acre.
  3. *Sandlot.* POV: Denis Kearney. Date: 1877. Opening image: torchlight and "The Chinese Must Go!"
  4. *Exclusion.* POV: Stanford in the Senate race. Date: 1882–85. Opening image: the Exclusion Act signed.
  5. *Yuma.* Hopkins dies in his rail car, Mar 28, 1878 (Rayner ch 12); Huntington: "My old friend Mr. Hopkins has gone over the river."
  6. *Santa Clara County.* Date: 1886, as a flash-forward tag only. Opening image: a court reporter's headnote.
- **Repo:** ch/2-10-law-as-armor.qmd, 2-11-exclusion-scapegoat.qmd.
- **Sources:** Rayner chs 9–13; White-R; Winkler chs 4–5; Saxton; Harris ch 1.2.
- **End turn:** Mar 1884, a telegram from Florence.
- **Reader realizes:** monopoly needs a scapegoat and a court, and it gets both.

## Episode 4 — The Boy and the Farm (1884–1905)
- **Cold open:** Feb 28, 1905, Moana Hotel, Honolulu. Jane Stanford: "This a horrible death to die" (White-J ch 29, p. 173). Then cut back 21 years.
- **Through-line:** grief turns a fortune into something that can't die.
- **Scenes:**
  1. *Florence.* POV: Jane. Date: Mar 13, 1884. Opening image: a fever room; the boy dies two months before turning 16 (Harris ch 1.3).
  2. *The Children of California.* Leland's vow, "The children of California shall be our children" (Harris intro).
  3. *Palo Alto Farm.* POV: Muybridge photographing trotters. Date: 1878, placed as an earlier memory. Opening image: a horse frozen mid-air (Harris ch 1.3).
  4. *Opening Day.* POV: a pioneer-class student (Herbert Hoover). Date: Oct 1, 1891.
  5. *The Widow Keeps It Alive.* POV: Jane in 1893–1898; the estate frozen in probate.
  6. *The Ross Affair.* POV: Jordan visiting George Howard on the night of Nov 14, 1900 (White-J ch 17, p. 101).
  7. *Poland Spring.* Jan 1905 (White-J ch 28ff).
  8. *Moana.* Back to the opening. The narrator's one sentence on the contested verdict. Jordan's "natural causes" (White-J ch 36, notes p. 339).
- **Repo:** ch/2-12-converting-monopoly-permanence.qmd, 2-13-widow-will-machine.qmd.
- **Sources:** White-J chs 10–45; Harris chs 1.3, 2.1 ("The Almost Certain Murder…").
- **End turn:** the casket goes into the mausoleum (White-J ch 45, p. 266). The founders are gone, the institution is untouchable, and a young engineer is already rich.
- **Reader realizes:** the university is the fortune made permanent and set beyond accountability. It was even able to manage its founder's death.

## Episode 5 — The Engineer's Century (1895–1945)
- **Cold open:** 1897, Western Australia. Hoover, 23, at the Sons of Gwalia mine.
- **Through-line:** a Stanford graduate turns engineering into statecraft; the university learns to rank people.
- **Scenes:**
  1. *Gwalia.* POV: Hoover. Date: 1897–1908 (Harris ch 2.3).
  2. *Bionomics.* POV: David Starr Jordan and Lewis Terman. Date: 1906–1916. Opening image: an IQ test sheet (Harris ch 2.2).
  3. *Earthquake.* Apr 18, 1906: the Memorial Church spire falls (Harris ch 2.1).
  4. *Feeding Belgium.* POV: Hoover. Date: 1914–1919. Opening image: an archive crate shipped to Stanford, the seed of the Hoover Library.
  5. *Commerce.* Hoover as secretary and then president, 1921–1933, ending with Hooverville.
  6. *Radio Lab.* POV: Fred Terman with Hewlett and Packard. Date: 1937–39. Opening image: the Addison Avenue garage.
  7. *Harvard Radio Research Lab / internment.* Date: 1942. Terman goes east, while Santa Clara Japanese families are taken away (Harris ch 2.4).
- **Repo:** ch/3-01 through 3-13 (stanford-prototype-hoover, mining-empire-frontier, eugenics-social-control, logistics-state, archive-as-power, credentialing-interface, witness-who-did-not-graduate [Steinbeck], depression-interpretation, total-war-filter, terman-research-university, university-contractor-state, california-reclassified, the-hand-off).
- **Sources:** Harris chs 2.1–2.4; Whyte, *Hoover* (Drive); Stern; Kevles; Bush, *Endless Frontier*.
- **End turn:** 1945. Terman comes home with a plan, and the land can't be sold.
- **Reader realizes:** the university now produces the people who run things and the method for ranking them.

## Episode 6 — The Park (1945–1969)
- **Cold open:** 1953. Varian signs the first Stanford Industrial Park lease; Terman sits on Varian's board (Harris ch 3.1).
- **Through-line:** land that can't be sold is leased to the future.
- **Scenes:**
  1. *The Lease.* POV: Terman. Date: 1951–53.
  2. *Shockley.* POV: Robert Noyce arriving in Mountain View. Date: 1956.
  3. *The Eight.* Shockley's phrase, "the traitorous eight" (Harris ch 3.2). Date: Sept 1957.
  4. *Defense Money.* Lockheed in Sunnyvale and the Minuteman program. Date: 1956–62.
  5. *East Palo Alto.* POV: a Black family barred from buying across the creek (Harris ch 3.1). Date: 1950s.
  6. *April Third.* POV: student occupiers of the Applied Electronics Lab. Date: Apr 3–9, 1969. Opening image: the poster "Strike Against the Stanford Empire" (Harris §III frontispiece).
- **Repo:** ch/4-01-termans-garden.qmd, 4-02-shockley-and-the-eight.qmd, 4-03-fairchild-platform-company.qmd, 4-04-defense-corridor.qmd, 4-07-april-third-movement.qmd.
- **Sources:** Harris chs 3.1–3.4; Berlin, *Man Behind the Microchip*; Leslie; Lowen; Lyman; NYT 1969; the a3m archive.
- **End turn:** Stanford cuts SRI loose in 1970, and the military work moves off-campus without slowing down.
- **Reader realizes:** the rebellion didn't stop the machine. It made the machine look less like a weapons lab.

## Episode 7 — The Road (1969–2002)
- **Cold open:** 1972, 3000 Sand Hill Road. Kleiner and Perkins open on Stanford-owned land (Berlin ch 15).
- **Through-line:** a road on university land becomes the gate every new company passes through.
- **Scenes:**
  1. *Kleiner & Perkins / Swanson and Genentech.* Date: 1974–76 (Berlin ch 15).
  2. *Pong and Atari.* POV: Al Alcorn. Date: 1972–79 (Berlin ch 20).
  3. *Reimers's Patent.* POV: Niels Reimers at Stanford's licensing office, patenting recombinant DNA; Bayh–Dole follows in 1980 (Berlin pt 3; Harris ch 4.2).
  4. *Nike.* POV: Phil Knight. Date: 1964–80. Repo 4-06.
  5. *Reagan's Cabinet.* Shultz and Weinberger, both Bechtel men, at the Bohemian Grove nexus (Denton chs 12, 24; one sourced detail only).
  6. *BackRub.* POV: Larry Page. Date: 1996–98. Jan 1997: Page emails Vinod Khosla a plan for Excite to buy BackRub for $1.6M, including $300,000 to Stanford for the license; Excite passes (Levy ch 1, “Excite’s total outlay would be $1.6 million, including $300,000 to Stanford for the license”). Stanford holds the PageRank patent.
  7. *The Garage.* Santa Margarita Ave., Menlo Park, rented to Google at $1,700 a month (Levy ch 1).
  8. *PayPal.* Date: 1998–2002 (Chafkin; Soni).
- **Repo:** ch/4-05-capital-chokepoint.qmd, 4-06-shoe-paper.qmd, 4-08-hollywood-parallel-platform.qmd, 4-09-pc-revolution.qmd, 4-10-bayh-dole-reagan.qmd, 4-11-internet-gold-rush.qmd, 4-12-google-attention-monopoly.qmd, 4-13-paypal-origin.qmd, 4-14-platforms-replace-infrastructure.qmd.
- **Sources:** Berlin chs 15–20; Mallaby; Levy chs 1–2; Harris chs 4.1–4.2; Denton; Chafkin; Soni; Knight.
- **End turn:** 2002. eBay buys PayPal, and the young men walk out with cash and a network.
- **Reader realizes:** the university now earns from its students' companies and owns the land their investors work on.

## Episode 8 — The Open Era (2002–2025)
- **Cold open:** Jan 20, 2025, the inauguration. Tech founders seated ahead of cabinet nominees. Open on the row of faces.
- **Then:** cut back to 2004.
- **Through-line:** the machine stops hiding.
- **Scenes:**
  1. *Facebook arrives in Palo Alto.* Date: 2004–2012. Sources: Levy, *Facebook* (needs mobi conversion); FTC 2019.
  2. *Palantir.* Date: 2003–2020. Sources: Chafkin.
  3. *Cambridge Analytica.* Date: 2014–18. Sources: Wylie.
  4. *The China Loop.* Sources: Sheehan.
  5. *The Rosewood on Sand Hill Road*, on Stanford land (Baker ch 6, p. 120).
  6. *A Student Reporter.* POV: Theo Baker, Nov 2022: "I called Sam, the Daily editor. 'Holy shit,'…" (Baker ch 4, p. 94). Tessier-Lavigne resigns in 2023 (special committee report).
  7. *Musk.* The Oval Office and then DOGE (Siddiqui chs 6, 11).
  8. *Compute.* The AI chokepoint, 2023–25.
  9. *Back to the inauguration.* Only now does the narrator name the pattern, once.
- **Repo:** ch/5-01 through 5-10 (scale-as-governance, china-california-loop, facebook-attention-economy, palantir-software-state, legitimation-machine, stanford-under-pressure, inauguration-spectacle, ai-final-chokepoint, moral-ledger, mechanism-proven).
- **Sources:** Baker; Siddiqui; Isaacson; Vance; Levy (*Plex*, *Facebook*); Wylie; Sheehan; Chafkin; Zuboff; stanford-special-committee-2023.
- **End turn:** none. The coda follows.
- **Reader realizes:** it was never a secret.
