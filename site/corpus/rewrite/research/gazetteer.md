# STANFORD STREET NAMES — COMPLETE GAZETTEER & HISTORICAL GUIDE  
Grounded in Richard W. Cottle, Stanford Street Names: A Pocket Guide (Stanford Historical Society, 2014) and Cardinal Chapter 5.11 / Appendix A

"Read a street sign as a document and it tells you four things: who, when, why, and who decided. The Peninsula's signs answer all four consistently: the campus is a map of who held the passage, in order."

================================================================================  
FIVE HISTORICAL LAYERS OF THE PENINSULA GRID  
================================================================================

LAYER 1: THE MISSION ROAD (1769–1834)  
- Galvez Street / Mall: José de Gálvez, inspector general of New Spain.  
- Junípero Serra Boulevard: Franciscan president of the mission system. Runs western edge of campus.  
- Lasuen Mall: Fermín de Lasuén, central campus axis designed by Olmsted.  
- El Camino Real: Spanish supply route linking missions. Peninsula main artery.

LAYER 2: THE RANCHOS (1834–1848)  
- Alvarado Row / Court: Juan Bautista Alvarado, Mexican governor.  
- Peter Coutts Road / Frenchman's Road: Jean-Baptiste Paulin Caperon, French banker who built Matadero Rancho, sold to Leland Stanford in 1881.  
- Ayrshire Farm Lane: Dairy farm on Coutts's ranch assembled into the Stanford Farm.

LAYER 3: THE RAILROAD BARONS (1848–1905)  
- Stanford Avenue & Leland Avenue: Leland Stanford, governor, senator, Central Pacific founder.  
- Jane Stanford Way: Replaced Serra Mall as main ceremonial quad entrance.  
- Mayfield & College Terrace streets: Named for early eastern universities (Columbia, Dartmouth, Harvard, Oberlin) platted by Alexander Gordon to capture Stanford land boom.

LAYER 4: THE TECHNOCRATIC ACADEMY (1905–1945)  
- Jordan Quad / Jordan Hall: David Starr Jordan, founding president and eugenicist.  
- Herbert Hoover Memorial Building & Hoover Tower: Herbert Hoover (Class of 1895).  
- Terman Engineering Center: Frederick Terman, provost and father of Silicon Valley.

LAYER 5: THE VENTURE CORRIDOR (1945–PRESENT)  
- Sand Hill Road: Global epicenter of institutional venture capital (Sequoia, Kleiner Perkins, Andreessen Horowitz).  
- Page Mill Road: Historic timber haul route turned tech corridor (HP, Synopsys).  
- Varian Way, Hewlett Way, Packard Drive: Corporate founders rooted directly in Stanford research leases.
