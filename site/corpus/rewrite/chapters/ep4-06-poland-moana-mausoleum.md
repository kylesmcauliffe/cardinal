# Episode 4: The Boy and the Farm

## Poland Spring, Moana, Mausoleum

The episode that began with Jane Stanford dying at the Moana must close with the same hotel, the same poison, and a casket sliding into stone. Between January and February 1905, fear crossed an ocean. Between February and March, David Starr Jordan crossed behind it.

January 1905. San Francisco. Nob Hill. Jane Stanford raised a bottle of Poland Spring water to her lips in her bedroom, tasted bitterness, and knew immediately that something was wrong.

She induced vomiting. She called her physician. Analysis of the water found strychnine—documented at the time as an attempted murder (repo ch 2-13). She told those close to her that someone in her circle meant to kill her. Fear and spiritualism, which had shadowed her since Florence, converged on a practical fact: poison in a labeled bottle from a trusted brand.

Investigation followed. Suspects were discussed. No satisfactory arrest closed the case. Jane did not stay to press the matter in every drawing room on the hill. She prepared to leave.

On February 15, 1905, roughly a month after the Poland Spring water incident, Bertha Berner and a maid, May Hunt, accompanied Jane Stanford on the steamship *Korea* bound for Honolulu (White 2022, ch. 28, 167). The ocean was meant to be distance. It was not safety.

### The second glass

February 28, 1905. The Moana Hotel on Waikiki. Address: 2365 Kalākaua Avenue. Jane Stanford took bicarbonate of soda before bed. Convulsions followed.

She cried out that she had been poisoned. Physicians attended. She spoke her last words within the hearing of those present.

"Oh God, forgive me my sins… Is my soul prepared to meet my dear ones?… This a horrible death to die" (White 2022, 173).

Attending physicians and a coroner's inquest concluded strychnine poisoning. Analysis found poison in the bicarbonate container (repo ch 2-13).

David Starr Jordan did not wait for the island's verdict to become the university's memory. He travelled to Honolulu. Timothy Hopkins came with him. "By the time David Starr Jordan and Timothy Hopkins reached Honolulu, it was apparent that all the suspects were lying" (White 2022, 217).

Jordan engaged another physician and produced an account attributing death to heart failure. The university adopted that account. A San Francisco headline would read: "Mrs. Stanford Died from Natural Causes" (White 2022, notes ch. 36, 339).

The coroner's jury named strychnine; the university's president named her heart; the chemists found poison in the body and the bicarbonate—"the combination of strychnine in both Mrs. Stanford's body and the bicarbonate seemed conclusive. She had been poisoned" (White 2022, ch. 36, 208).

Who administered it remains unknown on the surviving evidence. The chapter does not rest on naming a murderer. It rests on what the institution did with a founder's death.

### The Korea crossing

Twelve days at sea change routines. Jane walked decks. She read. She prayed. She did not forget January. When the *Korea* docked in Honolulu, she checked into the Moana with the staff who knew her habits—which glass she used, which powder she took for digestion.

Waikiki was not Nob Hill. It was sand, surf, and hotel service. Distance from San Francisco did not mean distance from danger if the killer traveled in memory or in another servant's loyalty.

### Control of the story

Jordan had told George Howard's faculty to "keep still" in November 1900 (White 2022, 101). In March 1905 he controlled a larger silence. On March 15, the day Jordan left Honolulu with the body, a man signing as "Tay Wang" wrote to Jordan asking for a meeting—"the strangest piece of evidence…" in White's account (White 2022, ch. 43, 248).

The name sat at the intersection of labor history and mystery. Chinese workers had built the line. Chinese servants worked in wealthy households. A letter in English asking the president for a meeting on the day the body sailed might mean anything or nothing. White treats it as uncanny. The chapter records it and does not solve it.

The letter did not stop the ship. The body's journey back to California did not reopen a criminal case the university preferred closed.

Malcolm Harris would later title a chapter with playful censorship: "The Almost Certain Murder of [asterisks]" (Harris 2023, ch. 2.1). The joke is the point. Everyone knew the question. The institution learned to asterisk it.

Jane had intended, according to some accounts in the research file, to dismiss Jordan upon her return from the islands. She did not return alive. The president who had complied in the Ross affair remained president who certified natural causes in Honolulu.

The self-perpetuating board continued. Trustees still replaced themselves. No governance clause changed because the widow died abroad.

### Back to the Moana

The cold open of this episode began in the same room where this chapter ends narratively—the trade wind, the palms, the convulsions. The reader has now walked the twenty-one years between Florence and that night: the vow, the grant, the farm's horse frozen in 1878, opening day in 1891 with Herbert Hoover in the pioneer class, probate and the fifteen-million-dollar suit Jane won, Ross expelled and Howard told to keep still, Poland Spring in January and the *Korea* in February.

The Moana was not a random hotel. It was where a wealthy woman could die with physicians nearby and reporters at a distance until Jordan arrived.

Her death did not close the university. It did not merge the institution with the state. It did not force land sales the Founding Grant forbade. It tested whether permanence meant surviving founders, and the test was passed the way probate had been passed: without structural change.

### The mausoleum

The body came home. Mourners gathered. The institution Jane had kept alive through panic and lawsuit buried its co-founder with ritual suited to a saint of California's civil religion.

The choir sang "Nearer, My God, to Thee," and the casket went into the steel chest in the mausoleum (White 2022, ch. 45, 266).

Steel chest. Mausoleum. The boy who died in Florence in 1884 already had his name on the university gate. Now the mother joined the architecture of memory on campus and in stone.

Leland Stanford had died in 1893. Jane Stanford died in 1905. Both founders were gone. The machine was intact—quieter, and more powerful, because it no longer depended on a living heir or a living widow to write checks from personal allowance.

### What did not happen

The university did not collapse. Governance did not democratize. Faculty who had resigned over Ross did not return en masse. Enrolment did not vanish. Jordan remained president until 1913.

Within a year the San Andreas Fault would test the campus physically. Buildings would fall. The church spire would collapse. The institution would rebuild because its wealth was land and endowment, not glass and tile alone (repo ch 2-13). That earthquake belongs to the next episode's opening weather. Here the point is simpler: founders died, poison or heart, lawsuit or silence, and the charter held.

### The young engineer, already rich

On the far side of the Pacific, a pioneer student from the class of 1891 was no longer sleeping in unfinished campus housing. Herbert Hoover, born 1874, had taken his geology training into mines in Western Australia and China (Harris 2023, ch. 2.3). By 1905 he was a young mining engineer accumulating the fortune that would later fund relief commissions, cabinet departments, a tower on the quad, and a library of war documents.

By 1901 he was a partner in Bewick, Moreing; by 1908 he was running his own firm (repo ch 3-01). He was not on the board yet—that cloudy appointment would come in 1912 (Whyte 2017, ch. 6). <!-- bib: TODO Whyte --> He was proof of what the opening day had promised: the university could produce a man who moved ore and capital on four continents while the founders' names moved into stone.

The orphans California claimed as children had grown into engineers the world could use. One of them was rich before he was famous, and the farm that had photographed a horse in mid-air had credentialed him.

### End of the episode

Episode 4 began with a horrible death in Honolulu and cut back twenty-one years to a boy dying in Florence. It ends with a casket sliding into a steel chest while a choir sings, and with a graduate in a mine shaft halfway around the world.

The Southern Pacific still owned the past. Stanford University owned a future it could not sell, governed by men who appointed themselves, and narrated even its founder's dying.

The founders were gone. The institution was untouchable.

The street signs were not yet twelve feet up and lit at night. That walk belongs to the coda of the series. Here the reader has watched grief become a grant, a grant become a campus, a campus survive probate and poison, and a professor's firing teach the nation a vocabulary Stanford did not want spoken on its farm.

Episode 5 opens in Western Australia, where the humanitarian's mine air would later be called "notably bad" and where the engineer king learned that organizing society like a cartel could look like virtue if the cartel fed Belgium.

### Poland Spring in detail

Poland Spring was a brand the wealthy trusted. Jane drank bottled water in an age when municipal supply was not always safe. Bitterness was not normal. Vomiting was a body's alarm.

The January analysis finding strychnine turned a bedroom into a crime scene. Police and private investigators asked who had access. Servants, secretaries, physicians, trustees—the circle was small and frightened.

Jane's decision to sail suggested she did not believe the danger had passed in San Francisco. She brought Bertha Berner and May Hunt because trusted staff were also suspects in some theories White explores. The ocean was escape and exposure at once.

### Honolulu physicians versus Jordan

Local doctors saw convulsions consistent with poison. They held autopsy. They convened a coroner's jury. Their unanimity is why later scholarship often favors murder over heart failure.

Jordan arrived with institutional weight. Timothy Hopkins accompanied him—nephew of the late Mark Hopkins, railroad associate, university insider (White 2022, 217). "All the suspects were lying" is White's noir summary of what they found when they began asking questions.

The Examiner headline "Mrs. Stanford Died from Natural Causes" (White 2022, notes ch. 36, 339) was the story Jordan wanted on the mainland. It competed with chemists who said otherwise (White 2022, ch. 36, 208).

One sentence settles it for this series: she was poisoned; who poisoned her is not known; the university preferred another diagnosis.

### Burial and continuity

The steel chest in the mausoleum (White 2022, ch. 45, 266) made permanence physical. Stone outlasts palm fronds. "Nearer, My God, to Thee" framed the widow as pilgrim, not victim.

Students mourned from a distance. Faculty returned to classes. Hoover mined. Jordan presided. Trustees appointed successors.

Episode 4 began in Honolulu and ends there in memory, with the casket lowered and a pioneer engineer rich on another shore.

The memorial church would rise and fall in earthquake. The mausoleum would remain. Visitors would walk campus paths named for founders and presidents without learning Jane's last words unless they read historians like White.

Harris would later joke about almost certain murder in a chapter title (Harris 2023, ch. 2.1). The joke is California's habit of knowing and not saying.

Jordan's natural-causes story would hold for decades in official accounts. Chemists' strychnine would hold in court records. The institution held both by choosing which file to teach.

When the steel chest closed, Leland and Jane Stanford were together in stone while their university continued without them—exactly what the grant had been written to allow.

Campus bells did not toll for Jane the way they would for a sitting president, but mourning rituals still marked her passing. Students who had never met the widow still walked paths she funded. Faculty who had feared her temper noted the silence.

The mausoleum scene—choir, hymn, steel chest—was choreography for permanence (White 2022, ch. 45, 266). "Nearer, My God, to Thee" framed grief as pilgrimage. The casket's metal container suggested indestructibility, as if poison in life could be sealed away in death.

Hoover in a mine shaft had no hymn. He had ore and profit. The pioneer class had scattered across extraction sites and offices while founders entered stone.

Episode 5 will follow him to Western Australia, where air in a mine would be called notably bad and where engineering would meet empire (Whyte 2017, ch. 5). <!-- bib: TODO Whyte --> Episode 4 ends with founders entombed and institutions unburied.

Bertha Berner survived to tell what she heard at the Moana. Jordan survived to shape the story on the mainland. Trustees survived to appoint each other. The founders did not survive, but the grant they signed did.

February 28, 1905, joined March 13, 1884, in the family's calendar of catastrophes. One death created the university. Another death tested whether the university could control its own story. The Moana's waves kept breaking after the steel chest closed.

January's Poland Spring bottle and February's bicarbonate jar would sit in evidence files while Jordan promoted heart failure on the mainland. Two containers, one poison, two coasts, one institution choosing its narrative.

Hoover mined on another ocean. Shockley was not yet born. The peninsula's industrial future was unthinkable in 1905. Yet the land clause in the 1885 grant already held the acreage that would later be leased rather than sold, as the Founding Grant required.

Jane Stanford's death closed the founder era. The charter era continued.

White's account of the *Korea* voyage places Bertha Berner and May Hunt beside a woman still afraid after January (White 2022, ch. 28, 167). White's account of March 15 places a letter from Tay Wang beside a president sailing with a body (White 2022, ch. 43, 248). The historian's job is to lay those facts side by side without forcing a culprit the record will not name.

The reader leaves Episode 4 with stone, steel, and a story still contested in headlines—but not in the chemists' report the series accepts in one sentence. Jordan's Examiner headline fades. Strychnine in the bicarbonate does not. The founders are buried. The grant is not. A young engineer already rich on another continent is the episode's last turn toward the future. Herbert Hoover had left the farm campus behind. The institution had not left him. Episode 5 picks up in his mines overseas.

The boy and the farm are finished. The machine is ready to think, and to rank, and to lease the land the Founding Grant of November 11, 1885, said it cannot sell.
