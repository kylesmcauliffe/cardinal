# CARDINAL — Cut Material Log

A record of what was removed from the inherited drafts and why. Kept so that
nothing disappears silently, and so that any decision here can be revisited
with the reasoning intact.

The governing standard is `voice.md`: Cardinal is not conspiratorial, not
hagiographic, not breathless, and it never builds a chapter on thin evidence.
Most of what follows was cut because it violates a rule the project set for
itself.

---

## 1. Events that appear to be invented or speculative

Presented in the drafts as narrative fact, with no sourcing, describing events
that cannot be verified.

| Draft material | Disposition |
|---|---|
| An assassination dated March 2025, named to a specific public figure | **Cut entirely.** Unverifiable as written. |
| "Dallas — March 15 2025," a scene rendered in specific detail | **Cut entirely.** No sourceable event. |
| Named engagement, affair, and scandal details for living technology executives | **Cut entirely.** See section 2. |
| Legislative and political events dated into late 2025 and beyond | **Cut pending verification.** Act IV now ends at events that can be sourced. |

A nonfiction book cannot carry unsourceable claims about living people. This is
both an evidentiary and a legal standard. Where the underlying subject matter is
genuinely relevant — political violence and its relationship to platform
incitement, for example — it is treated structurally, with sourced examples,
rather than through a specific unverifiable scene.

## 2. Tabloid material

The late chapters of the chronology drafts drift into gossip: Epstein
name-associations used as guilt by proximity, a Bezos affair, an alleged affair
between two technology founders, and similar.

**Cut.** The Voice Guide is explicit: "When the book is critical — and it is
critical — the criticism comes from evidence and structure, not from outrage.
Outrage ages. Evidence does not." Sexual gossip about living people does not
illuminate chokepoint control, does not advance the mechanism, and would
discredit the sourced material sitting next to it.

Where a relationship is genuinely load-bearing to the argument — Thiel's
$15 million backing of a Senate campaign that reached the vice presidency, for
instance — it stays, because it is a documented financial and political fact
that demonstrates elite network reproduction.

## 3. Conspiratorial framing

| Cut term | Replacement |
|---|---|
| "The Cardinal conspiracy" | Removed. The book's own thesis document says the mechanism is structural, "a more disturbing argument than conspiracy, and the true one." |
| "Cartel-polity" | Removed. |
| "The California Cartel" (chapter title) | Retitled "Scale as Governance: The New Chokepoints." |
| "Cardinal: The California Conspiracy" (working title) | Retired in favour of "The Subliminal History of Silicon Valley." |

"Cartel" asserts coordination. The evidence supports convergence — talent,
capital, and legitimacy routing through the same corridor because that is where
the returns are, and the returns being there because that is where they route.
The Voice Guide's own formulation: "Gravity is not a conspiracy."

## 4. The AI-authored epigraph

Every draft opens with a poem, "The Cardinal," followed by the sign-off
"Sincerely Yours, ChatGPT."

**Cut from the manuscript.** A work of nonfiction published under an author's
name cannot open with a machine-generated poem credited to the machine. The poem
is preserved at `_project/inherited-epigraph.md` for reference.

The author may write a replacement epigraph in his own voice, or the book may
open cold on the Introduction. The current build opens cold.

## 5. Contested history stated as settled

### Jane Stanford's death

The drafts state as fact that Jane Stanford was poisoned with strychnine in
Honolulu on 28 February 1905 and that the cause was recorded as heart failure to
protect the university.

**Retained, but reframed.** This is a genuinely contested question. There was a
strychnine incident in San Francisco weeks earlier; the Honolulu physicians'
finding and the university's subsequent account diverge; a body of later
scholarship argues for poisoning and a competing body argues the medical
evidence is inconclusive.

Voice Guide rule 2 governs: state the strongest version of each position, name
the uncertainty, make clear which reading Cardinal finds most persuasive and
why. The chapter's argument does not rest on the answer — Act I chapter 13
proves that the institution survived its founders regardless of how the last one
died, which is the more interesting and more defensible claim.

### Other contested items flagged for the same treatment

- The precise degree of Stanford's personal culpability for Chinese labour
  conditions on the Central Pacific, as distinct from Crocker's and Strobridge's.
- The causal weight of the Muybridge commission in the invention of cinema.
- Sterilisation totals under California's 1909 law: figures vary by source and
  by whether institutional records or retrospective studies are used. The book
  will cite a range with its basis rather than a single round number.

## 8. Scope moved out of this book

- **The Greek-to-Roman genealogy as two full opening chapters.** An early scheme
  opened the book with the Oracle of Delphi, Socrates, Plato, Aristotle and
  Alexander, then Caesar's succession and Augustus versus Caesarion. Compressed
  into Act I chapters 2 and 3. The book is a history of California; the deep
  genealogy earns its place only as pattern-setting, not as subject.
- **Trinity / time-traveller metafiction.** The drafts' own note is followed:
  all personal-narrative and metafiction elements belong to a different project.
  The traveller in the epilogue is a literary device with no name, backstory, or
  arc.
- **The Artometrics season and transmedia framing.** Out of scope for the
  manuscript.
