# The Campus Walk — street/building skeleton for Cardinal

Source of record for the street entries below: Richard W. Cottle, *Stanford
Street Names: A Pocket Guide* (Revised & Updated), Stanford Historical Society,
2014. Entries marked **[G]** were read directly from the guide (photographed
copy); entries marked **[A]** are carried from the manuscript's existing
Appendix A / general campus record and should be verified against the guide
before they anchor prose. Namesake dates and characterizations from the guide
are reproduced here as the guide gives them and are subject to the project's
normal sourcing standard.

The organizing idea: **the campus is a map of who held the passage, in order.**
Every street is an artifact. Read in sequence they reconstruct the whole
argument of the book — Spanish crown → mission → rancho → railroad fortune →
research university → defense corridor → venture capital → platform/AI donor
era — inside a single forty-square-mile walk. This file is the reference the
re-outlined chapters and Appendix A draw on.

---

## Layer 0 — the crown and the idea of the mission (the Gálvez origin)

This is the origin the book now hands its opening to: not Serra first, but the
*administrator* who commissioned Serra.

- **Galvez Street / Galvez Mall** [G] — **José de Gálvez (1720–1787)**, inspector
  general of New Spain. In 1767 he implemented the royal decree **expelling the
  Jesuits** from New Spain; in 1768, under Carlos III, he went to Baja California
  and there appointed **Gaspar de Portolá** military commander and **Junípero
  Serra** religious leader of the Alta California expedition. *The man who had the
  idea for the missions.* Runs between El Camino Real and Serra Street. → opens
  Act I; the administrative origin point.
- **Junípero Serra Boulevard** [G] — **Father Junípero Serra**, Franciscan
  president of the mission system, "founder of nine California missions." Western
  edge of campus. → mission-system chapter (2-01).
- **Lasuen Street / Lasuen Mall** [A/G] — **Fermín de Lasuén**, Serra's successor
  as mission president (verify entry). The central campus axis (East–West Axis /
  Lasuen Mall, laid out by Olmsted, 1919). → mission continuity; also the
  Olmsted campus plan.
- **Alvarado Row / Alvarado Court** [G] — **Juan Bautista Alvarado (1809–1882)**,
  leader of the 1836 California revolt against Mexican centralization and
  **governor of Alta California, 1836–42**; his grandfather was on the Portolá
  expedition of 1769. Name chosen by David Starr Jordan. → thin-sovereignty /
  Mexican California (2-03, 2-04).
- **Argüello** family [G, left-column reference] — early Spanish/Mexican
  Californio governing family; land/patent title thread (verify entry). →
  rancho-and-title chapter (2-04).
- **Mendoza Road** [G, via Granada entry] — **Don Antonio de Mendoza**, first
  viceroy of New Spain. → the crown's administrative reach.
- **El Camino Real** [A] — the Spanish supply route linking the missions a day
  apart; still the Peninsula's main street. → 2-02.

## Layer 1 — the rancho and the land assembly (1834–1882)

How the Spanish/Mexican land became one private estate — the physical
precondition for everything after.

- **Peter Coutts Road / Peter Coutts Circle; Frenchman's Road; French Lake
  Drive** [G] — **Jean-Baptiste Paulin Caperon** ("Peter Coutts," the "mysterious
  Frenchman"), a wealthy banker, bibliophile, and self-described socialist who
  emigrated from France in 1874, acquired **Matadero Rancho** in Mayfield in 1875,
  built a 1,400-acre stock farm and dairy, then in 1881 sold the estate to
  **Leland Stanford for $140,000**. → the land-assembly chapter (2-04 / 2-12).
- **Ayrshire Farm Lane** [G] — the Ayrshire Dairy Farm on Coutts's Matadero
  Rancho, 1875–81; **Leland Stanford purchased the land in 1882**. → same.
- **Rancho San Francisquito / Rancho de las Pulgas** [A] — Mexican-era grants
  assembled into the Stanford farm and San Mateo County place names.
- **Governor's Avenue / Governor's Lane / Governor's Avenue South** [G] — a
  1.4-mile path along the **Palo Alto Stock Farm's trotting track**, from the
  1870s; the "governor" is **Leland Stanford**, governor of California. Eucalyptus,
  later plane trees and pin oaks. → the fortune arrives (bridges 2-09 railroad →
  2-12 university).
- **Electioneer** (street) [G, back-cover teaser] — Leland Stanford's champion
  **trotting stallion** at the Palo Alto Stock Farm; the horse-breeding operation
  that produced Eadweard Muybridge's motion studies. → the fortune's leisure face;
  a good museum object.

## Layer 2 — the railroad fortune becomes an institution (1885–1905)

- **Stanford** (the campus, the name) [A] — Leland and Jane Stanford; the Founding
  Grant's eight thousand acres that forbade sale. → 2-12, 2-13.
- **Museum Way** [G] — the museum founded in memory of **Leland Stanford Jr.**, an
  avid collector; **Jane Stanford** its de facto director and major donor;
  damaged 1906 and 1989; reopened 1999 as the **Iris & B. Gerald Cantor Center
  for Visual Arts**. → the memorial logic; donor-naming across a century (2-13,
  IV.5).
- **Palm Drive** [G] — called University Avenue in the early days; the ceremonial
  entry to the Quad; Memorial Arch (destroyed 1906) stood at its head; autos
  banned on it until 1914. → the front door; the museum walk's opening step.

## Layer 3 — the research university and its builders (1891–1955)

- **Jordan** (formerly Jordan Hall; streets named *by* him) [A/G] — **David Starr
  Jordan**, first president; ichthyologist, pacifist, and **eugenicist**; personally
  named many campus streets (Alvarado Row, etc.). Building renamed 2020. →
  eugenics/first-president chapter (3-03), and the naming power itself.
- **Dudley Lane** [G] — **William Russell Dudley (1849–1911)**, botany professor,
  Cornell classmate of Jordan, redwoods-conservation advocate; the **Dudley
  Herbarium** (650,000 specimens, merged into the Cal Academy of Sciences, 1976).
- **Durand Way** [G] — **William Frederick Durand**, engineering professor, U.S.
  Naval Academy graduate; work on **propulsion / jet propulsion**; national honors
  (1948). → engineering → defense pipeline (3-11, 4-04).
- **Samuel Morris Way** [G] — **Samuel Brooks Morris (1890–1962)**, class of 1911,
  Stanford's **second dean of engineering**, then **general manager and chief
  engineer of the Los Angeles Department of Water and Power**; builder of the
  Morris Dam; the street dedicated 1933 by **Herbert Hoover**; bordered the Terman
  Engineering Center (demolished 2012). → engineering-runs-the-infrastructure
  thread.
- **Nathan Abbott Way** [G] — **Nathan Abbott (1854–1941)**, came to Stanford in
  1893 to **establish the Law School**. → the credentialing/legal-armor thread
  (2-10, 3-06).
- **Roth Way** [G] — **Almon E. Roth (1886–1964)**, class of 1909, ASSU president,
  university controller (1919–37), trustee; secured land/financing for the
  Stanford Golf Course and Frost Amphitheater; later ran the Pacific American
  Steamship Owners Association. → the administrative machine.
- **Rosse** (street) [G] — economist of **mass communication**; later president/COO
  of Freedom Communications (*Orange County Register*). → media-economics thread.
- **Mosher** (Clelia Duel Mosher) [G] — pioneering researcher on women's health;
  medical investigator in France in WWI. → an inclusion/counter-current case.
- **Encina, Roble, Branner, Toyon, Lagunita, Escondido, Lomita, Salvatierra,
  Santa Teresa, Panama, Serra, Gerona, Alturas, Campus Drive, Mayfield,
  Arguello, Pine** [A/G] — residence-hall, Spanish-revival, and ring-road names
  (many read in the guide); the everyday texture of the walk. Verify individual
  entries as they are used.

## Layer 4 — the defense corridor and the Research Park (1946– )

- **Hansen Way** [G] — **William Webster Hansen**, physicist; runs from **Page Mill
  Road** to El Camino through the **Stanford Research Park**; associated with the
  **klystron / microwave** work that seeded **Varian Associates** and Stanford's
  defense-electronics industry; Hansen died of beryllium exposure. → Terman's
  garden / defense corridor (4-01, 4-04).
- **Page Mill Road** [A] — spine of the **Stanford Research Park** (opened 1951); a
  lumber road before it was an industrial one. → 4-01.
- **Porter Drive** [G] — off Page Mill; the guide's entry ties the area to
  **Frederick E. Terman** and electrical engineering. → 4-01.
- **Sand Hill Road** [A] — the capital corridor; Kleiner Perkins and Sequoia
  (both 1972), minutes from campus. → 4-05.
- **Coyote Hill Road** [A] — **Xerox PARC**, 1970. → 4-09.

## Layer 5 — the donor era (1970– )

- **Knight Management Center / Knight Way** [G/A] — **Phil Knight**, Nike; the GSB
  complex, dedicated 2011, funded by the author of a 1962 Stanford class paper
  about importing Japanese running shoes. → the "shoe paper" chapter (4-06) and
  the donor-naming tariff (IV.5).
- **Avery Mall** [G] — the **Avery family** (GSB 1948; Avery Construction); named
  for a major athletics gift. → the donor era's ordinary form.
- **Cantor** (Museum Way, above) [G] — Iris & B. Gerald Cantor. → IV.5.
- **Munger Graduate Residences** [G, cover note] — Charlie Munger (Berkshire). →
  IV.5.
- Donor-named buildings, institutes, and professorships generally [A] — "the
  current layer, allocated at published tariffs."

## The absences (the negative that carries IV.11)

- **The Muwekma Ohlone** — territory the campus occupies; very little named for
  them, and recent.
- **The Chinese workforce of the Central Pacific** — no major thoroughfare, no
  campus building; commemorations recent and descendant-led.
- **The people sterilised under the 1909 law** — nothing.
- **Sam McDonald** [G] — a rare, telling *inclusion*: a long-serving Black Stanford
  employee for whom Sam McDonald Park (Santa Cruz Mountains) and a road are named;
  a genuine and specific counter-example to weigh honestly.
