# CARDINAL — Draft Source Inventory

An audit of the seventeen uploaded files (twelve unique after de-duplication)
that preceded this manuscript, recording what each contained, what was mined
from it, and what was discarded.

Total prior material: roughly 150,000 words across five mutually incompatible
structural schemes.

## Prose drafts — mined, not preserved

### The world-history run (three identical copies)

`CARDINAL_full_bc97.txt`, `CARDINAL_df53.txt`, `CARDINAL_950e.pdf`

Same text distributed across three containers. Roughly 18,000 words of finished
prose covering the Late Bronze Age through Qing China, plus ~8,900 words on
Spanish and Mexican California through 1890.

**Mined for:** Act I entirely; Act I chapters 1–7 and 10.

**Problems corrected:**

- Chapter 8 exists twice, in two full versions (~1,830 and ~1,450 words) on the
  same period. Resolved in favour of the longer, with the institutional-engine
  material from the shorter folded in.
- Chapters 2 and 3 are absent from `CARDINAL_full_bc97.txt`; the numbering jumps
  from 1 to 4. Present in `CARDINAL_df53.txt`.
- Chapter 1 is titled "c. 1300–1000 BCE" but its content runs to Augustus, 27 BCE.
- Act I Chapter 9 ends mid-sentence: "The next step would be social: stabilizing".
- Chapter 10 carries a note saying it will stop before the Atlantic revolutions;
  Chapter 11 then covers them in full.
- Editorial residue throughout: "Your transcript," "Below is Chapter 5,"
  "If you want, I'll write Chapter 6 next," "Excellent. Here is Chapter 7."
- `CARDINAL_df53.txt` opens with a "Primer on Time Travel" (~2,500 words). Its
  substance — bioeconomics, systems within systems, reading history the way an
  ecologist reads a food chain — is absorbed into the Introduction. The
  time-travel conceit is retained only as the epilogue's organising device.

### The American Odyssey draft

`Cardinal_2_6ddf.pdf` — dated 2025-06-25, 131 pages, ~30,900 words

Twenty-five finished chapters plus one truncated, covering 1848–1950 in a
65-chapter / 5-act scheme. A different and more metaphor-forward prose voice
than the world-history run: "imperial core," "root node," "refactored the
nation's source code."

**Mined for:** Act II above all — this is the only draft with real prose on
Hoover's return, the Hoover Institution's founding, the OSS, Vannevar Bush's
campus visit, the Radio Research Laboratory, Operation Paperclip, and the
Industrial Park groundbreaking. Also strong on the 1906 earthquake and the
"campus apart" geography argument.

**Problems corrected:**

- Chapter 9 (Jane Stanford's death) breaks off mid-word: "protecting her univer".
- Chapters 27–65 are empty placeholders reading "Write chapter content here."
- The voice runs hotter than the Voice Guide allows. Re-registered on the way in.

### The chronology bibles

`CARDINAL__1__7da9.pdf` (dated 2025-10-08, 133 pages, ~35,300 words) and
`CARDINAL_2_efa7.txt` (~32,200 words)

Overlapping bullet-form research covering 1848–2025 across 64 chapters and 62
sections respectively. Densest factual coverage of the modern era anywhere in
the drafts.

**Mined for:** Acts IV and V — semiconductors, venture capital, the personal
computer, the dot-com era, PayPal, the platform era, AI. Also the Chinatown
street geography and the Bank of America / Giannini thread.

**Problems corrected:**

- Chapter 39 reads "TO BE ADDED"; chapters 47 and 48 are titled only "7" and "8."
- Part IV is titled 1899–1945 but its chapters begin in 1949.
- Part VI is titled 1975–1989 but runs through 9/11 and PayPal.
- Chapters 52 and 53 are internally labelled "Chapter 4" and "Chapter 5."
- Two chapters both cover 2020–2025.
- Back matter is Quarto template boilerplate: an appendix reading "In summary,
  this book has no content whatsoever" and a single Knuth citation on literate
  programming. Both were artefacts of the unedited starter template, not
  intentional content.
- Substantial unverifiable and tabloid material. See `cut-material.md`.

## Coverage Gaps Found

Present in none of the drafts, and now commissioned:

- **Why the railroad ran where it ran.** Every draft asserts the Pacific Railroad
  Act of 1862 without explaining the sectional deadlock it resolved. This is the
  causal hinge between the Civil War and Stanford's existence. Now Act I, ch. 8.
- **John Steinbeck.** Absent entirely. He attended Stanford 1919–1925 and left
  without a degree. Now Act II, ch. 7.
- **Phil Knight and Nike.** Nike appears only as an offshore supply chain;
  Knight is never named. He took a Stanford MBA in 1962 and wrote Nike as a
  class paper. Now Act III, ch. 6.
- **Internal opposition in the Valley era.** No draft covers the campus
  anti-war movement against Stanford's own military research. Now Act III, ch. 7.
- **Any bibliography at all.** No draft has one. The Event Ledger carries source
  tags `[S1]`–`[S87]` with no key. Phase 6.
