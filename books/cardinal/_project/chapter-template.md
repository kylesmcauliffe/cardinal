# CARDINAL — Chapter Draft Template

### Standard Structure for All 60 Chapters

-----

## How to Use This Template

Every chapter in Cardinal is built on the same skeleton. The skeleton is invisible in the finished prose — the reader never sees it — but it is always there. Before drafting any chapter, fill out the Pre-Draft Brief below. Then follow the structural sequence. Then run the quality checks.

This is not a formula. It is a discipline.

-----

## PART ONE: PRE-DRAFT BRIEF

*(Fill this out before writing a single sentence of prose)*

**Chapter:** [Act number, Chapter number, Title]

**Core Question:** The one question this chapter answers. Should fit in one sentence. If it takes two sentences, the chapter is trying to do too much.

**The Answer:** The conclusion the chapter lands on. Write the last sentence of the chapter before writing the first.

**The Mechanism:** How does this chapter illuminate the book’s central engine — choke point control, public subsidy to private monopoly, elite network reproduction? Be specific. If you cannot connect this chapter to the mechanism, the chapter may not belong in the book.

**The Key Fact:** The single most important piece of evidence in this chapter. The one thing the reader must walk away knowing.

**The Human Anchor:** The person (or people) who makes this chapter feel like a story rather than a lecture. Cardinal is structural analysis told through human beings. Name the anchor character.

**Excluded Voices:** Who is excluded, exploited, or erased in this chapter’s period? This does not need to be a separate section — it should be woven into the analysis — but it must be tracked.

**Proof this chapter is ready:** Complete this sentence — *“This chapter proves that __________ by showing __________.”* If you cannot complete it, stop. Revise the core question first.

-----

## PART TWO: CHAPTER STRUCTURE

### SECTION 1 — THE HOOK (1–3 paragraphs)

**Function:** Earn the reader’s attention immediately. Start with the most interesting, specific, or surprising fact in the chapter. Do not build to it. Open with it.

**Rules:**

- Start with a specific fact, date, person, or scene — not a generalization
- No “In the year…” or “Throughout history…” openings
- No more than three paragraphs before the reader knows what this chapter is about
- The hook should imply the core question without stating it yet

**Test:** If the first sentence were the only sentence a reader saw, would they want to read the next one?

-----

### SECTION 2 — THE CONTEXT (2–4 paragraphs)

**Function:** Give the reader exactly what they need to understand what follows — no more, no less. Context is not background. It is the minimum structural information required for the argument to land.

**Rules:**

- Assume the reader is intelligent but not specialized
- Explain the *significance* of things, not just the things themselves
- Use the zoom-in / zoom-out rhythm: specific → structural → back to story
- Do not rehearse what the previous chapter already established

**Test:** Could any of these paragraphs be cut without the chapter losing anything essential? If yes, cut them.

-----

### SECTION 3 — THE ARGUMENT (the chapter’s body, variable length)

**Function:** Build the case. Present the evidence. Let the mechanism become visible.

**Rules:**

- Each paragraph advances the argument — it does not decorate it
- Facts lead; interpretation follows; never the reverse
- Every claim that is not common knowledge needs a source (tracked in Research Log, not embedded in prose)
- The bioeconomic lens is always operating: who controls inputs, who captures surplus, who is excluded, how the system reproduces
- Specificity is the argument: dates, dollar amounts, addresses, names, quotes
- The human anchor character appears here — their choices, their context, their role in the mechanism

**Structural pattern within the argument:**

1. State the fact or event
2. Show its structural significance
3. Connect it to the mechanism
4. Move to the next fact

**The “Spite Fence” test:** Every major fact should be able to move between micro (the specific anecdote) and macro (the structural meaning) in the same breath. If a fact only operates at one level, either develop it further or cut it.

-----

### SECTION 4 — THE EXCLUDED (1–2 paragraphs, woven in or standalone)

**Function:** Account for who paid the cost of the power being described.

**Rules:**

- Not a separate “social justice” section — integrated into the structural analysis
- Not performative or moralistic — stated plainly and connected to the mechanism
- The question to answer: who was excluded or exploited in this chapter, and how did that exclusion make the mechanism work?
- Chinese railroad workers, Indigenous dispossession, scapegoated immigrants, gig workers, content moderators — the specific people matter

**Test:** If this section were removed, would the chapter’s argument be incomplete? It should be.

-----

### SECTION 5 — THE LANDING (1–2 paragraphs)

**Function:** Answer the core question. Close the door. Hand off to the next chapter.

**Rules:**

- The landing states the conclusion the chapter has earned — not a summary, not a list of what was covered
- One clean, declarative statement of what this chapter proved
- The last sentence should either close completely or open directly onto the next chapter’s question — no dangling threads
- No “In conclusion…” or “As we have seen…”

**Transition rule:** The final paragraph of every chapter should make the next chapter feel inevitable. Not by predicting it — by creating the logical condition that makes it necessary.

**Test:** Read only the first paragraph and the last paragraph of the chapter. Do they form a complete argument? If yes, the chapter is structurally sound.

-----

## PART THREE: QUALITY CHECKS

Run these before the chapter leaves draft status.

**Voice checks:**

- [ ] Does every paragraph pass the three-way conversation test (generalist / expert / skeptic)?
- [ ] Is there any passive voice hiding an agent of action? Find it and fix it.
- [ ] Is there any hedging where the evidence is clear? Cut it.
- [ ] Is there any outrage where evidence would do more work? Replace it.
- [ ] Does the chapter sound like the Voice & Style Guide examples?

**Structural checks:**

- [ ] Does the chapter answer its core question?
- [ ] Does the chapter illuminate the mechanism?
- [ ] Is the excluded group present and connected to the structural argument?
- [ ] Does the opening start with a specific fact, not a generalization?
- [ ] Does the landing earn its conclusion?
- [ ] Is the transition to the next chapter logical, not decorative?

**Fact checks:**

- [ ] Is every specific claim (date, dollar amount, name, address) logged in the Research Log with its source?
- [ ] Is any claim built on Wikipedia as a primary source? (If yes, find the original source.)
- [ ] Is any claim contested by historians? (If yes, is the contest acknowledged and handled per the Voice & Style Guide rules?)

**The thesis check:**

- [ ] Read the Thesis One-Pager. Does this chapter advance the book’s central argument?
- [ ] If a reader skipped this chapter, would the book’s argument still hold? (If yes, the chapter needs to work harder or be cut.)

## PART FOUR: EXAMPLE — TEMPLATE APPLIED

**Chapter:** Act I, Chapter 8 — Railroads and the First California Monopoly (1861–1869)

**Core Question:** How does infrastructure become sovereignty?

**The Answer:** The railroad does not just move goods — it sets the terms under which goods can move at all. Whoever owns the tracks governs the economy.

**The Mechanism:** Classic choke point control. Federal subsidy (Pacific Railroad Act land grants + bonds) converted into private monopoly (the Contract and Finance Company skimming profits). The excluded: 10,000+ Chinese laborers who built the system and were denied citizenship, political protection, and equitable wages.

**The Key Fact:** The Big Four’s Contract and Finance Company paid itself inflated sums for every mile of track built — the railroad was a vehicle for extracting public money into private hands before the first train ran.

**The Human Anchor:** Collis P. Huntington — specifically his letter admitting “It is my business to buy Congress.” He embodies the mechanism without pretense.

**Excluded Voices:** Chinese laborers — paid less, excluded from skilled positions, denied political protection. Named and connected to the argument that California grows powerful by excluding the workers who make its systems possible.

-----

**HOOK:** Huntington’s letter. One sentence. The reader knows immediately what kind of chapter this is.

**CONTEXT:** The Pacific Railroad Act of 1862 — what it offered, why it was structured the way it was, what “federal land grants” actually meant in acreage and dollars.

**ARGUMENT:** Contract and Finance Company structure → land grant scale → political capture → Chinese labor system → rate monopoly after completion → “The Octopus” as a name that arrives organically from the evidence.

**EXCLUDED:** Chinese laborers — built the system, denied its rewards, later made the scapegoat for wage depression by the same elites who benefited from their labor.

**LANDING:** “Rail controlled movement. But what the Big Four really controlled was the terms under which California could participate in its own economy. That lesson — control the chokepoint, set the terms — would not end with steel and steam. It would be translated into new forms. The next translation would be called a university.”
