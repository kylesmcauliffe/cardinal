# Episode 5 — Research Questions

## Questions

1. What did Hoover actually do at Sons of Gwalia, and what did the 1905 Royal Commission find about working conditions there?
2. How did Branner’s patronage shape Hoover’s path from conditional admit to trustee?
3. How did Jordan’s eugenics relate to California’s 1909 sterilisation law and to the university’s legitimacy?
4. What did Lewis Terman’s Stanford-Binet revision change about Binet’s original purpose?
5. What broke on campus on 18 April 1906, and why could the institution absorb the loss?
6. How did the Commission for Relief in Belgium operate across blockade and occupation, and what records did Hoover send to Stanford?
7. What did Hoover do in office during the Depression, and why did “Hooverville” stick?
8. What did Terman tell Hewlett and Packard in 1939, and why did Stanford miss the largest wartime contracts?
9. What did Terman run at Harvard’s Radio Research Laboratory, and what plan did he bring back in 1945?
10. What happened to Japanese American families in Santa Clara County under Executive Order 9066, and how does that sit beside Terman’s war work?

## Answers (with sources)

1. Hoover surveyed, managed, and accounted for the Sons of Gwalia mine at Coolgardie; the 1905 Royal Commission found the air “notably bad” (Whyte 2021, 100; Harris 2023, ch. 2.3; `books/cardinal/ch/3-01`, `3-02`).
2. Branner paid Hoover’s typhoid medical bills; Hoover met Lou Henry in Branner’s geology lab (Harris 2023, ch. 2.3; Whyte 2021, 43; `moments.md` Ep 5).
3. Jordan chaired eugenics committees and advocated hereditary explanations for social quality; California’s 1909 sterilisation law authorized non-consensual operations in state institutions (`books/cardinal/ch/3-03`; Harris 2023, ch. 2.2).
4. Terman’s 1916 scale produced a single inherited-capacity score for mass use; army testing followed in WWI (`books/cardinal/ch/3-06`).
5. Fault rupture near campus destroyed library, gym, arch, church spire; two dead; university reopened autumn 1906 because endowment and land, not buildings, were the core assets (`books/cardinal/ch/2-13`; Harris 2023, ch. 2.1).
6. CRB negotiated with both sides, ran ships and books; 1919 Hoover funded Adams to collect war/revolution documents in Europe (`books/cardinal/ch/3-04`, `3-05`).
7. Hoover expanded federal intervention (RFC, public works, wage conferences) but failed politically; Bonus Army footage ended him; Roosevelt won on narrative (`books/cardinal/ch/3-08`).
8. Terman loaned and advised HP to stay; NDRC/OSRD sent major contracts to MIT/Harvard/Caltech/Bell, not Stanford in 1940 (`books/cardinal/ch/3-09`).
9. Terman directed Harvard RRL on radar countermeasures (~800 staff); returned 1946 with “steeples of excellence” strategy and lease insight (`books/cardinal/ch/3-10`; Harris 2023, ch. 2.4).
10. ~120,000 incarcerated west coast; property sold or lost; Santa Clara County farms/businesses affected (Harris 2023, ch. 2.4; `books/cardinal/ch/3-09`).
