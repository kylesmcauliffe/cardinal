# Cardinal Notes — Rayner, The Associates

The moments below are in reading order, with the earliest chapter first. More will be appended as reading continues.

- (Ch 2) “Death is carrying off the Americans most fearfully,” Huntington wrote to his brother. “There are eight to ten deaths a day.”
  - Why: Huntington stuck in the mud of Panama in 1849: our toll-man starts as a stranded traveler. Episode 2.
- (Ch 9) Paraphrase: Huntington fears the Wilson Committee “would demand to see the Contract and Finance Company books.”
  - Why: Hidden books; the end-turn of Ep 2. Episode 2/3.
- (Ch 12) Hopkins dies in his rail car at a siding in Yuma, Mar 28, 1878; Huntington: “My old friend Mr. Hopkins has gone over the river.”
  - Why: A tycoon dying alone in his private car. Episode 3.
- (Ch 13) The railroad gets its patents to San Joaquin lands, then announces prices “ranging from $17 to $40 an acre.” This leads to Mussel Slough.
  - Why: A price notice that becomes a gunfight. Episode 3. [Rayner + standard histories]
- (Ch 17) Quoting Norris’s The Octopus: “California likes to be fooled.”
  - Why: A line the narrator can hold in reserve. Episode 3.
- (Notes, ch 3) Judah, Sacramento Union, Nov 9, 1860: “I have struck a lucky streak.”
  - Why: The surveyor's optimism before he is pushed out. Episode 2.
